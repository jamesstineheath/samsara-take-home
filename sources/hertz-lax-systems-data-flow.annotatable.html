Systems and Data Flow for an Asset Uptime Planner

A map of how the Hertz LAX maintenance supervisor's day-in-life connects to existing systems, data objects, output loops, gaps, and feasible product boundaries.

Representative customer: Hertz LAX Primary user: Fleet Services Supervisor Frame: Asset Uptime Status: research artifact for annotation Stops before metrics

Core Read

The job is Asset Uptime.

At Hertz LAX, the local version of asset uptime is revenue-readiness: returning the right vehicles to rentable inventory while demand is moving.

The raw maintenance objects mostly exist.

Samsara already claims work orders, PMs, faults, inventory, POs, vendors, invoices, warranty, and asset history. Competitors also cover these objects.

The gap is reconciliation.

The product opportunity is joining maintenance truth, rental-operations truth, and local shop truth into a plan the supervisor can inspect and commit.

The Three Truths

Maintenance Truth

What is broken, due, open, diagnosed, blocked, or complete.

  • Work orders and PMs
  • Faults, odometer, usage
  • Parts, warranty, recalls
  • Vendor work and invoices

Rental-Operations Truth

Which cars are needed, available, rentable, rentable soon, or unavailable.

  • Reservations by class
  • Ready-line inventory
  • Clean, fuel, charge state
  • Return and damage holds

Local Shop Truth

What the supervisor and technicians know that systems often miss.

  • Who is actually on shift
  • Bay and yard reality
  • Tech skill and speed
  • What can safely wait

Asset Uptime planner

Reconciles the three truths. Separates ready, blocked, vendor, defer, and needs-review work.

  • Explains why work matters today

Predictive Loop: Where It Breaks Now

A predictive maintenance product is not a line from input data to recommendation. It is a loop: the system senses, predicts, recommends, gets human commitment, drives execution, observes the result, and learns whether asset uptime improved.

1. Sense

Collect telematics, PMs, WOs, returns, inspections, parts, vendors, tech roster, ready-line state, and demand.

2. Predict

Estimate risk, priority, readiness, downtime, return-to-service time, and asset-uptime impact.

3. Recommend

Draft the plan, blocked reasons, assignments, deferrals, and ops-facing availability forecast.

4. Commit

Supervisor reviews, edits, approves, overrides, and owns the final plan.

5. Execute

Technicians, parts owners, vendors, and rental operations act on the committed plan.

6. Observe

Capture blockers, time, parts, new findings, status, and whether the asset returned to service.

7. Learn

Update future estimates, confidence, routing rules, blocked reasons, and recommendations.

Breakpoint What Breaks Why It Matters Product Implication
Identity join The same vehicle, work order, return record, vendor estimate, and ready-line state do not resolve cleanly to one asset state. The planner cannot tell whether an asset is ready, blocked, duplicated, or stale. Show asset-state reconciliation and conflict flags.
Signal quality Faults, complaints, inspection notes, and scans can be noisy, vague, duplicated, or low confidence. Prediction can create false urgency or miss the real issue. Show source, confidence, severity, evidence, and needs-review states.
Missing local context Tech skill, bay state, physical location, informal parts reservation, and can-wait judgment stay in heads or on whiteboards. The model can recommend work that cannot actually start. Capture only the minimum local context needed for readiness.
Output actionability A ranked list says what matters but not who should do it, what is needed, why today, or what done means. The supervisor still has to translate prediction into work. Output a committed plan and technician-ready work cards, not just risk scores.
Human trust The supervisor cannot inspect reasoning, tradeoffs, or uncertainty. They ignore the recommendation or recreate the plan manually. Require review and commit. Make overrides easy and capture why.
Execution drift A part is missing, a tech finds extra work, a vendor delays, or operations changes demand. The original plan becomes stale quickly. Treat the plan as a living loop with blocker updates and plan-impact alerts.
Closeout quality Work closes with sparse notes, missing parts, missing time, or unclear asset status. The system cannot learn which recommendations were right. Capture outcome labels: done, blocked, deferred, returned to service, not returned, reason.
Ops feedback Maintenance marks work done, but rental operations still sees the asset as unavailable, unclean, uncharged, or unstaged. Asset uptime is not achieved until the asset is usable. The loop must end in an availability state, not just work-order completion.
Learning target The model optimizes for closing work orders instead of improving asset uptime. The product can look productive while missing the business outcome. Train and measure against ready, available, downtime avoided, and forecast accuracy.

Existing Tools and Data Objects

System or Tool What It Likely Owns Gap for the Planner
Samsara telematics / IoT GPS, odometer, diagnostics, faults, usage, possibly EV state. Faults and diagnostics do not equal a daily shop plan.
Samsara Connected Maintenance / CMMS Work orders, PMs, asset history, costs, inventory, vendors, invoices, warranty, POs. Generic work-order status does not say whether work is ready, valuable today, or aligned to rental demand.
Hertz rental operations Reservations, vehicle class demand, customer wait pressure, fleet availability, swaps. Critical for prioritization, but likely outside a CMMS. Needs integration or mocked input in prototype.
Ready-line / lot operations Clean, fueled, charged, staged, physically available, or held. On site is not the same as rentable. This data may be split across tools and local knowledge.
Return inspection and damage capture Customer issue, condition, mileage, photos, automated scan findings. Signal quality varies. Findings need normalization before becoming work.
Parts / inventory / procurement On-hand quantity, location, consumption, POs, low-stock and out-of-stock status. The planner needs confidence and reservation state, not just theoretical quantity.
Vendor / dealer / approved repair path Approved repair location, voucher, estimate, ETA, invoice, authorization changes. Real-time ETA and estimate status may be weak unless integrated. Manual status can work in v1.
Supervisor whiteboard and memory Priority, informal constraints, team skill, bay reality, can-wait judgment. This is the hardest source. V1 should capture only the minimum local context needed to make a plan.

Day-In-Life Mapped to Systems

4:45 to 5:30 AM

Pre-shift scan

Supervisor question: What changed overnight, and what can we return to revenue today?

Systems: Samsara/CMMS, rental operations, ready-line, vendor updates, local whiteboard.

Gap: Inputs describe the same vehicles from different angles, but no system naturally says which vehicles are most worth moving today.

Prototype feasibility: high
5:30 to 6:20 AM

Plan and huddle

Supervisor question: What work should each tech start, and what should operations believe about availability?

Systems: Work-order system, technician app or paper, inventory, supervisor judgment.

Gap: A priority queue is not enough. The plan needs readiness, sequence, and a reason.

Prototype feasibility: high
6:20 to 10:00 AM

First execution wave

Supervisor question: Is the plan still true now that work has started?

Systems: Technician updates, CMMS status, return/check-in tools, vendor communication.

Gap: Missing parts, new findings, and vendor delays can stale the plan fast.

Prototype feasibility: medium-high
10:00 AM to 1:00 PM

Revenue-readiness checkpoint

Supervisor question: What can still make it back to the ready line today?

Systems: CMMS, technician status, ready-line inventory, vendor ETAs, parts/PO status.

Gap: Maintenance tools show status, but the supervisor needs a forecast by revenue impact.

Prototype feasibility: medium
1:00 to 4:00 PM

Afternoon pressure

Supervisor question: What changes because demand, returns, or diagnosis changed?

Systems: Rental ops, return inspection, CMMS, vendors, whiteboard or huddle.

Gap: Replanning needs visible tradeoffs, not hidden automatic rescheduling.

Prototype feasibility: medium
4:00 to 5:30 PM

Shift handoff

Supervisor question: What must the next shift know so the same work does not get rediscovered tomorrow?

Systems: Work orders, technician notes, ready-line status, whiteboard, vendor/parts updates.

Gap: If blockers and next actions are not structured, tomorrow starts with archaeology.

Prototype feasibility: high

Gap and Feasibility Register

Gap Why It Matters at Hertz LAX Feasibility Practical V1 Approach
Rentable status, not just asset status A car can be on site but not rentable because it is dirty, unfueled, uncharged, damaged, held, or waiting on final check. Medium-high Add return-to-revenue state: ready now, ready after turn, ready after repair, blocked, unavailable today.
Vehicle class demand The same repair has different priority if SUVs, EVs, or vans are short today. Medium Mock or integrate reservation demand by class. Show why class pressure changes priority.
Return inspection normalization Customer comments, return-agent notes, and automated scans are noisy but operationally important. Medium Convert signals into reviewable issues with source, confidence, photo/note, and needs-review state.
Technician availability and skill The best plan is useless if the right technician is not on shift. High Lightweight roster: on shift, skill tags, current load, unavailable. Avoid full HR scheduling.
Bay and yard reality Work cannot start if the car is not accessible, the bay is full, or it is still in wash/charge/vendor. Medium-high Simple local state: on lot, in bay, at wash, charging, at vendor, blocked location unknown.
Vendor ETA and approvals Vendor/dealer work can dominate availability, especially collision, warranty, recall, and specialty work. Medium-low Start with manual ETA/status, uploaded estimates, vouchers, and invoices. Add vendor APIs later.
AI trust and alert noise Fault codes and inspections can create false urgency. Medium Show source, severity, confidence, evidence, and why today. Require supervisor commit.

Product Boundary Implied by the Mapping

V1 should be a supervisor-owned Asset Uptime planner. For Hertz LAX, that means a revenue-readiness loop: prepare the plan, separate ready work from blocked work, generate technician-ready assignments, observe outcomes, and update availability.

Samsara can plausibly own

  • Reconciling maintenance signals into a daily plan
  • Identifying ready versus blocked work
  • Drafting technician work cards
  • Explaining why an item is prioritized
  • Producing an ops-facing availability summary
  • Capturing carryover and blockers

Later or lower-feasibility

  • Deep vendor/dealer real-time integration
  • Automated parts ordering
  • Autonomous repair assignment without supervisor review
  • Repair-versus-retire optimization
  • Full replacement of rental operations or ERP systems
