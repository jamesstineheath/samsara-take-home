Connected Maintenance / Backend Architecture

Prepared Asset Brief: system design and agentic loop

A backend architecture for turning Work Orders, faults, DVIRs, PM schedules, parts, warranty, vendor, demand, and local shop context into ranked asset recommendations that a supervisor can verify and commit.

Work Orders stay system of record Asset-first recommendations Human approval before writeback No fake source buttons
Architecture intent

Three backend jobs

The product should feel like ordinary Connected Maintenance. The intelligence sits behind the Work Orders tab and prepares a short, ranked review flow.

Keep Work Orders as the source of execution

The parent tab remains Work Orders. The brief adds prepared recommendations and badges, but it does not become a separate planning system.

Prepare asset-level decisions before the shift

The backend ranks vehicles by uptime impact, feasibility, risk, and horizon: this shift, next 24-48 hours, or weekend surge.

Close the loop with supervisor judgment

Confirm, reject, defer, and commit actions become structured feedback. That feedback improves future recommendations.

Agentic architecture

Use agents for synthesis, not unchecked mutation

The architecture can be implemented as one service with modular steps. The key is the loop: collect signals, resolve asset state, prepare recommendations, verify assumptions, commit only after supervisor approval.

1

Collect signals

Pull WOs, faults, DVIRs, PMs, parts, demand, vendors, and shift context.

2

Resolve asset state

Join records by vehicle and mark knowns, unknowns, blockers, and stale data.

3

Plan recommendations

Rank asset decisions and draft WO updates with impact, tradeoff, and guardrails.

4

Verify assumptions

Route local floor truth to Confirm / Reject. Show source links only when they exist.

5

Write back safely

Apply approved WO changes with idempotency, audit trail, and current-version checks.

Deterministic rules

Safety flags, sourceability, inventory counts, stale data, permissions, idempotency, and writeback checks should be rule-based and auditable.

Agentic synthesis

Agents are useful for summarizing tradeoffs, reconciling messy notes, drafting WO amendments, and explaining why an asset is worth supervisor attention now.

Sourceability contract

Every assumption declares what kind of evidence exists

This is the backend rule that keeps the UI honest. A check can be important even when it has no source record.

System record

Known source exists

Example: brake pads staged in inventory or a tech is on the schedule.

UI: Source + Confirm / Reject.

Partial record

Record may be stale

Example: vendor ETA exists but has not been updated today.

UI: Source with stale warning.

Local truth

Floor check required

Example: Bay 04 is physically open or a vehicle is accessible.

UI: Confirm / Reject only.

Partner record

Outside Samsara

Example: dealer, tire vendor, glass vendor, or parts supplier status.

UI: Partner link if integrated.

Unavailable

No reliable record

Example: supervisor judgment, informal floor note, or untracked bay status.

UI: Confirm / Reject only.

Data model

The brief joins source data around the asset

Vehicles are the top-level decision unit. Work Orders are the execution layer underneath the vehicle.

Asset

Vehicle identity, class, location, status, availability, and history.

Work Order

Current execution record: status, source, severity, estimate, assignment, parts.

Source Event

Fault, DVIR, PM schedule, inspection, note, invoice, vendor update.

Inventory State

On-hand quantity, reservations, staged parts, open PO, expected arrival.

Demand Window

Class demand, target ready count, current ready count, next peak.

Prepared Brief

One generated brief for site, shift, horizon, and supervisor workflow.

Recommendation

Ranked asset decision with impact, action, affected WOs, checks, and state.

Outcome Event

Commit, rejection, defer, vehicle ready, WO reopened, or blocker found.

Writeback safety

The commit path should be intentionally boring

Prepared updates can be smart. Mutations should be conservative, audited, and easy to reverse or explain.

1

Supervisor commits

User approves one prepared update after checks pass.

2

Verify checks

Required assumptions are confirmed or explicitly overridden.

3

Check WO version

Prevent stale commits if the WO changed during review.

4

Apply update

Write with idempotency key and permission check.

5

Audit trail

Store before, after, actor, source recommendation, and timestamp.

6

Measure outcome

Track whether the vehicle returned to ready or the WO stalled.

Measurement

Metrics the backend can actually emit

These are measurable because they tie to concrete review, commit, rejection, writeback, and outcome events.

Prepared recommendation yield

Committed recommendations divided by surfaced recommendations.

False-ready-to-start rate

Assigned work that later stalls on parts, bay, access, staffing, or source quality.

Local check rejection rate

Rejected assumption checks divided by reviewed assumption checks.

Uptime recovered proxy

Vehicles returned to rentable status, weighted by demand pressure and time window.
